## Problem

Four visual directions for the panel were built as prototypes in `prototypes/`
and none of them has been chosen. The prototypes are good work — each one
covers the same two screens with the same data drawn from the real contract
enums, so they can be compared honestly — but a prototype that nobody picks
is a prototype that decided nothing.

Meanwhile the panel is being built anyway. WI-004 is putting the waiting list
on the landing page right now, and its front end is written in whatever
design language the app already has. That work is not wasted, but it is
_frozen_: every decision from here on is provisional until a direction exists.

The direction is also the standing question the factory asked and did not get
an answer to. In `.factory/INTAKE.md`:

> **Which visual direction does the panel take?** Four directions are built as
> prototypes in `prototypes/` and none of them has been chosen. The landing
> page (WI-004) is written in the current design language, so a direction
> chosen afterwards means rewriting its front end. Deciding late costs a
> rewrite; deciding early costs nothing that has been built, because the
> server side of WI-004 does not depend on the visual language.

"Late" is now. WI-004's front end exists and is uncommitted; a direction
chosen after that front end is finished means redoing it.

## Why now

Two reasons, and only one of them is about the deadline.

**The owner's wants point at a direction.** The wants are in
`.factory/INTAKE.md`, in the owner's words:

- W-1: "I see everything waiting on me in one place" — one list, not three.
- W-2: interaction — talking to agents — is the job that should get genuinely
  better.

Those two wants are a specification for a home screen, and the four
prototypes answer them differently. Deck puts an event stream at the centre
and every surface is an inspector over it. Desk leads with what needs me in a
single scroll. Orbit draws the system around the owner. Forge splits the
product into an orientation room and an execution room. A direction chosen
without asking "which one answers 'what needs me' in one place" would be
chosen on taste, and taste is not a reason the factory can put in a work
item.

**The cost of the rewrite grows with every front-end line.** Every screen
Cog builds on top of WI-004's list inherits the current design language.
Choosing now means the rest of the panel is built once.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **One direction is recommended, and the others are rejected.** A
      recommendation that does not say what it rejected, and why, is not a
      decision.
- [ ] The reasoning is tied to the owner's own wants — W-1, W-2, and the
      stated scope — and to what the prototypes actually do with the landing
      page WI-004 is building. Preference is not reasoning; "it looks better"
      is not an argument that survives a reviewer.
- [ ] If the recommendation is **Forge (Direction D)**, it settles the
      boundary Forge leaves open: D puts the map and the dashboards in one
      room and the conversation in the other, and the factory board, the agent
      list and the automation runs could reasonably belong to either. A
      recommendation that inherits an open boundary is not a decision. If D is
      rejected instead, the rejection says why.
- [ ] The cost of adopting the recommended direction **now** is stated
      concretely: which parts of the existing app are affected, what in
      WI-004's front end is preserved, and what is reworked. "Low effort" is
      not a cost; a list of what changes is.
- [ ] The recommendation is written where the next agent will find it:
      `prototypes/DIRECTION.md`, next to the prototypes it describes.
- [ ] The owner is asked one question with a recommended answer, so the
      decision takes one word — confirm, or name a different direction. The
      question is put to the owner; the factory does not assume the answer.
- [ ] The recommendation is marked as a recommendation, not a decision, until
      the owner answers. A direction the factory picked on its own is not the
      owner's direction.

## Notes

**The four bets, from `prototypes/README.md`:**

- **A — Deck.** Dark ops console. One pane, no page hierarchy; the event
  stream is the spine and every surface is an inspector over it. The
  conversation view adds a trace gutter showing the execution alongside the
  transcript — tool calls, deltas, artifact creation, spend — which the
  current UI hides entirely.
- **B — Desk.** Warm paper, light, Today-first. One vertical scroll
  answering what needs me, what is running, what exists. A reading-width
  conversation column with a plain-language side panel.
- **C — Orbit.** Deep indigo, aurora light, glass. The home is a
  constellation: the owner at the centre, agents in orbit, each thread drawn
  as the subgraph its agents share.
- **D — Forge.** The hybrid. Orientation room (map, dashboards) in C's
  spatial language; execution room (conversation, trace) in A's dense
  language. One material across both: dark base, hairline borders, violet for
  structure, cyan for data, amber always means live or waiting on you.

**The costs the prototypes already name.** B is the cheapest to build and
closest to the existing stack. A changes how the product behaves most,
because it promotes the event stream from an implementation detail to the
primary navigation surface. C introduces a genuinely new component, the
constellation graph, and is the hardest to keep legible as the number of
agents and threads grows. D is the hybrid to build if A and C are both partly
right.

**The tiebreak, said out loud.** `*.md` and `docs/decisions/` belong to
Wren. `prototypes/DIRECTION.md` is not product documentation — it is a
design artefact sitting beside the prototypes it describes, in a directory
that is excluded from prettier and is not part of any workspace build. It is
Vision's to write. The ADR that records the owner's answer is a different
document and a different owner: `docs/decisions/0007-*.md` is Wren's, and it
is written after the owner confirms, not before.

**Where the landing page is.** WI-004's front end is
`apps/web/src/components/WaitingList.tsx` and the landing route is
`apps/web/src/pages/Conversations.tsx`. Both are Cog's and uncommitted.

**What is real and what is not.** The prototypes use the real field names and
enum values from `packages/contracts` and `apps/server/src/db/schema.ts`, and
no screen shows a state the product cannot produce. But the screens are
static: nothing is fetched, nothing is wired, and the records are mock. A
recommendation that leans on a prototype's live behaviour is leaning on
something that does not exist.

**The owner has been asked.** The direction is the one open question from
the owner, and it has been asked once already. This item produces the
recommendation so that the question can be answered in one word instead of
four.
