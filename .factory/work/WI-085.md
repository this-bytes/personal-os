# WI-085 — The independent attack on the autonomy policy

**Owner:** Probe
**Verifier:** Chief, on Probe's own report
**State:** implement — dispatched 2026-10-21, in parallel with WI-082.
**Numbered:** 2026-10-21 by the session that owns the number space for this push.

## The problem

WI-082 makes the autonomy policy enforceable. It is the largest change this
factory has made to the execution path, and it is the change where a quiet
wrong answer is most expensive: a policy that is enforced wrongly is either a
policy that blocks work it should allow, or one that permits work it should
stop.

Under the ordinary rule the implementer does not write the check that accepts
its own work. **For WI-082 the owner has relaxed that rule**, so that the
implementation is not held up by a second round-trip. The cost of the
relaxation is that WI-082 needs an independent attack, and this is it.

## What this item does

Write the adversarial test for the autonomy policy, independently, against the
published decisions in WI-082 — not against whatever Cog happens to have
written.

## The decisions under test (these are settled)

1. One pure resolver in `packages/contracts/src/` resolves the effective policy
   from workspace + goal + conversation. Every enforcement point consumes that
   one result.
2. **Strictest wins.** Numeric limits take the minimum across workspace, goal
   and conversation. `allowedActions` is the intersection. `approvalRequired` is
   the union. The effective `mode` is the *least* autonomous of the three. A
   goal may tighten its workspace and never loosen it.
3. **Deny by default.** An action name outside the known vocabulary is denied.
4. **Manual must not regress today.** Owner-directed turns, owner-approved
   automations and triggered automations keep working. `manual` means the
   agent may not *begin* work on its own initiative.
5. The effective policy is readable.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `apps/worker/src/__tests__/autonomy-policy-attack.test.ts` exists and
      attacks the policy from the decisions above, not from the implementation.
- [ ] It covers, at minimum: a goal that tries to loosen its workspace is
      refused; an action outside the vocabulary is denied; `allowedActions`
      intersects rather than unions; `approvalRequired` unions rather than
      intersects; the least-autonomous mode wins; a turn that exceeds a limit
      is stopped and recorded rather than silently truncated.
- [ ] It covers the **regression** half: with every workspace on its default
      `manual`, an owner-directed chat turn, a factory task and a triggered
      automation still work.
- [ ] It is a *different file* from WI-082's test. The two must not collide.
- [ ] It does not weaken, skip or delete any existing test, and it does not
      edit a test it did not write.

## The rule that matters here

**If Cog's implementation differs from what you tested, say so — do not weaken
the test to match the code.** A red test against a wrong implementation is the
finding. Report the difference and let Chief decide which is right.

## Gates

## Deliverable

The test file, the gate output, and a report that names every place the
implementation and the published decisions disagree — including the places
where the implementation is simply not there yet.
