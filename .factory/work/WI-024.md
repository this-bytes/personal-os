## The problem this item solves

The panel is being built anyway, in the design language that exists today.
WI-004 put the waiting list on the landing page, and every front-end line
built on top of it inherits that language. Forge is now the direction, and the
adoption carries **structure as well as material**: two rooms, two densities, a
new component, a third surface, a room switch, and a material that re-hues
rather than replaces.

Forge lands as **one visible change**. Light tokens with dark cards and dark
page backgrounds read as broken, not as transitional — so the palette pass and
the structure pass are the same pass, and a panel that is half dark and half
light is not a Forge panel in progress, it is a broken one.

## What this item covers

1. **Three surfaces and a room switch.** Map / Work / Dashboards.
   `AppShell.tsx` changes shape, not just colour. Three routes, one material,
   two densities.
2. **The material.** Forge's variable block — `--bg`, `--surface`, `--border`,
   `--text`, `--muted`, `--faint`, `--accent`, `--live`, `--ok`, `--warn`,
   `--err`, `--info`. The neutral scale re-hues in place, so most call sites
   do not move.
3. **The execution trace.** `tool.started`, `tool.completed`,
   `artifact.created`, `execution.status`, and spend as an abstract counter
   (`spendUnits`). All of it is already in the contracts, so the trace is a
   surface over existing data.
4. **The dashboards screen.** Widgets bound to a data source's payload, with
   the bindings visible rather than implied. The contracts already carry
   `dashboardSpec` (widgets, bindings, `refreshSeconds`) and `dataSourceSpec`
   (`json`, `http`, `sqlite`).
5. **The finished control in the Map's Needs-you rail.** WI-004's control is
   already built. The adoption wraps it. The rail **carries the decision
   controls it actually has**: the owner approves and rejects in place. It does
   **not** carry acceptance — acceptance is decided on the factory board
   (`apps/web/src/components/TaskBoard.tsx:310-329`), reached from the rail only
   by a link, and no open item owns it.
   **This text is corrected on 2026-10-21.** It used to say the owner
   "approves, rejects or accepts in place, and never leaves the landing screen
   to decide". Probe's verification of WI-010 found that promise false as it
   stands — the rail carries Approve/Reject only
   (`apps/web/src/components/WaitingList.tsx:243-262`) — and the third authority
   is promised by the direction document and owned by nobody (D5). The brief
   says what the rail can do. **Building an acceptance control on the rail is a
   separate item, and it needs the owner.**
6. **Per-agent identity hues.** One `--agent-*` token per agent connection. The
   app has no equivalent concept today, and it lines up with WI-006's persona
   work: an agent has a name, a role and now a hue.
7. **The constellation.** An SVG graph of agents and threads, with a live spoke
   for agents working now, and a thread drawn as the subgraph its agents share.
   The data is real — agent connections, conversations, participants — but the
   prototype hand-places the nodes, so the build needs a real layout algorithm
   and a legibility rule for growth. This is the hardest piece of any of the
   four directions and Forge does not avoid it; it inherits it from Orbit.

## One item, and six briefs inside it

`prototypes/DIRECTION.md` says the constellation, the dashboards screen, the
trace and the per-agent hues are **each a build item with its own owner**. The
other Chief session said the same about the constellation: it probably wants
its own owner and its own brief, the way the trace gutter did.

**This item is the adoption, and it carries six briefs inside it.** The
adoption cannot be split across items and landed in pieces, because Forge
lands as one visible change. But one implementer cannot safely hold a layout
algorithm, a palette pass, three surfaces, a trace, a dashboard screen and a
set of agent hues — and the honest cost of this direction is that it is going
to the person with the longest queue.

So the item's first deliverable is six briefs — one per piece above — each
saying what it changes, what it preserves, who owns it, and the order it
lands in. The briefs are what let the adoption be executed by several owners
in sequence, without ever shipping half a Forge. **A brief is written before
the code that the brief describes.**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **The cost is stated concretely against the tree**, not as "low effort":
      which files and call sites change, what in WI-004's landing page is
      preserved, and what is reworked. The waiting list preserves everything
      that is not visual language — one request to `GET /api/waiting`, the
      derived list, the event-driven invalidation, the "one thing is one
      entry" rule, and the loading, empty and error states.
- [ ] **The palette numbers are reproduced from the tree, not copied from the
      direction document.** `git grep -c -E '<token>' -- 'apps/web/src'
'packages/ui/src'` for each of `slate-`, `indigo-`, `amber-`, `red-`,
      `emerald-`, `violet-` and `cyan-`. The direction document says 215
      `slate-` lines survive a re-hue in place, 34 `indigo-` lines move to
      violet, and amber is the only semantic collision at ten lines across six
      files — of which **four mean caution, not liveness**:
      `ConversationDetail.tsx:349` and `:504`, `ResourceEditor.tsx:263` and
      `:264`. Those four become neutral or `--err`; the fix is semantic, not
      chromatic.
- [ ] **The settled boundary is the one in `prototypes/DIRECTION.md`**, applied
      by the rule — _a picture of the system is orientation, a place where work
      is inspected or decided is execution_. The constellation and the agent
      list are in the orientation room (the constellation **is** the agent
      list, spatialised, and building it twice would build the same information
      twice); the factory board and the automation runs are in the execution
      room; the dashboards are in the orientation room.
- [ ] **The connect decision has a home.** The owner's Q2 answer makes
      connecting an agent a trust decision, and the agent list is now a
      picture in the orientation room — a room where, as a rule, nothing is
      decided. The Needs-you rail answers this for the waiting list, and the
      panel must not end up able to _list_ an agent and not _connect_ one,
      which is the same defect shape WI-004 was returned for. So the
      constellation carries the connect decision in place, or the agent list
      keeps a decision surface in the execution room. Which one it is, and
      why, is stated in the item.
- [ ] **The decision control is reused, not rebuilt.** WI-004's control is
      taken as it is; what changes is the layout it sits in. The landing page
      moves out of the root into the execution room, because the landing page
      is the decision list and the decision list belongs where decisions are
      taken.
- [ ] **Six briefs are written before the code they describe**, one per piece
      of the scope, each naming what it changes, what it preserves, its owner,
      and the order it lands in.
- [ ] **Two rules travel with the direction**: one accent with one meaning,
      and **no ratio whose denominator is zero** — the counters band shows the
      counts (N features, M operations) and a ratio only when there is a
      denominator to divide by.
- [ ] **Nothing outside the web client changes.** No server, no
      `packages/contracts`, no database, no CI, no dependency or lockfile
      change. Nothing in this direction changes behaviour, so nothing in it
      needs a contract change.
- [ ] The gates pass: `npm run typecheck`, `npm run test`, `npm run
format:check`, `npm run build`. The web suite covers the new surfaces,
      and the new component has the test that would have caught it missing.

## Not in scope

- **The undo surface.** The second half of the owner's authority — _show me
  what happened, let me take it back_ — has no surface in the product today.
  It needs its own brief and its own owner: authority without reversibility
  is exposure, because with one owner there is no second operator to absorb a
  bad action.
- **Any behaviour change.** A direction changes how the panel looks and how it
  is laid out, not what it does. The trace, the constellation, the dashboard
  screen and the agent hues are surfaces over data the product already keeps.
- **The data model, the server, the contracts.** The waiting list is derived
  on the server; nothing here changes how it is derived.
- **The decision control.** WI-004 owns it and it is done. This item wraps a
  finished control; it does not rebuild it.
- **A fifth direction.** The four prototypes were the candidates.

## Notes

**The honest cost, in the item rather than in a footnote.** One implementer,
with items already ahead of it and two more in verify, and this is the
largest build of the four directions. That is why the item carries six briefs
instead of one long task: the work can be spread across owners once the briefs
exist.

**What the owner gave up, stated plainly.** The factory recommended Desk, and
the reason was not taste — Desk is the only direction whose home screen _is_
the decision list, and the owner's own Q2 answer is that the human decides.
The owner chose the direction that recommendation rejected. This item is the
price of that, priced honestly: a new component, a third surface, a room
switch, and a material that re-hues rather than replaces.

**The risk that comes with it.** In Forge the map is the hero and the list is
beside it. Desk led with the decision list, so W-1 was the page; Forge leads
with the constellation, so W-1 is a rail. An owner whose loop is "scan the
list and act" will find Forge's landing screen decorative before they find it
useful. The mitigation that carries it is that **the rail decides in place** —
the landing screen is an inbox, not a map with a notification badge. That
mitigation is written into this item rather than left implied in a prototype,
and criterion 5 is where it is held.

**Size this against the tree, not against the document.** The direction record
is being verified by Probe on [WI-010](WI-010.md) at the same time as this
item opens, so a number in `prototypes/DIRECTION.md` may move by a line or
two. The cost model is reproduced from the tree, and that is criterion 2.
