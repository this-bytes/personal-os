## Problem

A turn, execution or task whose outcome cannot be confirmed is recorded as
**`failed`**.

**Before this item.** The three lines below are that tree's — `9ad4f98^`, the
commit before this item's first pass, which is where every one of them was
verified:

- `messageStatusSchema` was `streaming | complete | failed`
  (`packages/contracts/src/conversation.ts:56` at `9ad4f98^`; it carries
  `'unknown'` today, at `:62`);
- `executionStatusSchema` was
  `queued | running | blocked | completed | failed | cancelled`
  (`packages/contracts/src/agent.ts:78-86` at `9ad4f98^`; `:153` today, and it
  carries `'unknown'`);
- `taskStatusSchema` was
  `open | active | blocked | review | done | cancelled`
  (`packages/contracts/src/factory.ts:25-32` at `9ad4f98^`; `:25-33` today,
  with `'unknown'`).

`failed` reads as "safe to retry". An item that may already have had its
side effect can therefore be run twice — a double charge, a duplicated
deploy, a duplicated message to a real person.

**A concrete instance, verified in source, at `9ad4f98^`.** The worker aborts
a turn when the adapter's timeout elapses
(`apps/worker/src/executor.ts:217-218`), the abort throws, the throw is caught
and assigned to `failure` (`executor.ts:265`), and the turn is then recorded as
`failed ? { status: 'failed' } : { status: 'completed' }`
(`executor.ts:355`). A turn that timed out is a turn whose side effect may
well have happened. It is recorded as `failed`.

A second instance: **an approval that the owner rejects** is recorded as a
`failed` execution —
`resolveExecution(executionId, body.approved ? 'completed' : 'failed', …)`
(`apps/server/src/api/approvals.ts:44-46` at `9ad4f98^`). A rejection is not a
failure of the work; the work was correctly stopped.

The concept is already understood in this codebase —
`agentConnectionStatusSchema` carries `unknown`
(`packages/contracts/src/agent.ts:52-59` at `9ad4f98^`; `:127-134` today). It
was simply never carried onto work.

## Why now

The owner's whole picture of "what needs me" rests on the panel telling the
truth about each in-flight item. One mislabelled item is how a side effect
runs twice, and the owner is the one who has to live with it.

This is also the plugin's first rule, verbatim: _"Unknown is not a confirmed
failure: do not automatically redispatch."_ and _"Completed side effects are
not rolled back. Unconfirmed cancellation stays unknown."_

**The order matters and the window is open now.** WI-004 ("the landing page
shows what is waiting on the owner") renders the waiting list, and its front
end is currently **held** and uncommitted. If `unknown` lands before that
front end is finished, the waiting list learns to say "check this" once
instead of twice. If it lands after, the waiting list needs a second pass.
Holding WI-004 has accidentally opened the window this item needs.

this and we cannot confirm how it ended"**. It is never auto-retried, and

- The progress-based pause, routing, or the persona layer.
- Any change to how an approval is decided — that is WI-007.

### 1. `unknown` still becomes a retry — two defects

**Defect 1a — the factory-task and automation paths tell the worker
`failed`.** `apps/worker/src/executor.ts:811-815` and `:1002-1006` both
return:

```ts
return failure
  ? { status: 'failed', error: failure }
  : unknown
    ? { status: 'unknown', error: explanation }
    : { status: 'completed' };
```

The `failure` test comes **first**. On a deadline the catch sets `failure`
(`executor.ts:723-724`), and an `execution.error` sets it too
(`executor.ts:721`, `:885`), so the outcome the worker acts on is `failed`
while the database says `unknown`. The worker then calls
`queue.fail(job.id, …)`, and `Queue.fail` requeues while attempts remain —
so the work runs again.

The turn path gets this right (`executor.ts:559-563`): its booleans come from
the outcome, which tests `timedOut` first.

**Defect 1b — the reaper requeues a job whose item is already `unknown`.**
`Queue.reapStaleJobs()` (`apps/server/src/queue/client.ts`) sets `running`
jobs with a stale lease back to `queued`. The worker holds a job only after
the executor has returned (`apps/worker/src/index.ts:68`, `:79`), and the
executor records `unknown` inside the call — so a worker that dies in between
leaves the job `running` with the item already `unknown`, and the reaper
requeues it. This one happens on the turn path too, where the outcome itself
is correct.

`Queue.hold()` is sound wherever it is reached: the job becomes `unknown`,
`lockedAt` is nulled, the reaper only touches `running`, and `claimNext` only
takes `queued`. The two paths above never get there.
