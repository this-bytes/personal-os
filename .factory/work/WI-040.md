## The problem

**The front door is the product's promise.** The owner settled that on
2026-10-21: the owner leaves the panel to learn what the agent did. Today the
owner leaves it to press **Approve** or **Reject**
(`apps/web/src/components/WaitingList.tsx:272-284`) with nothing else in front of
them — not what the agent did, not what it produced, not what it cost.

**Forge settled where the evidence belongs.** _If it is a picture of the system,
it is in the orientation room; if it is a place where work is inspected or
decided, it is in the execution room — with one declared exception, the
Needs-you rail, which carries decisions in the orientation room because a
decision is taken where the list is_ (`prototypes/DIRECTION.md:45-48`). The
needs-you rail carries the decision controls, so the evidence comes to the
decision — and the same evidence is reachable where the work is read, so a
decision and its evidence are never two places apart.

**The control is not blocked by the layout.** The inbox is already a component,
and a layout change re-lays-out the list's _layout_, not the control inside it.
What changes with Forge is larger — and it is **settled, not open**: the root
route is the **Map** (`prototypes/README.md:24`), and the **Needs-you rail
sits on it and carries the decision controls**, because the rail is the rule's
one declared exception (`prototypes/DIRECTION.md:45-48`, `:58-66`). The
decision list does not move to the execution room, and the front door does not
move out of the root. That is **WI-024's** work, and this item does not touch
it.

      did, what it produced, and what it cost.** They come from the published
      evidence — WI-039's read — and from nowhere else.
- [ ] **The context is scoped to the item.** The drawer is about the thing
      being decided, not about the run in general, and it does not carry

      and its evidence are never two places apart. Today that is the

      than showing an empty box that reads as a failure.

      publishes. If it turns out to need a route of its own, that is a new

## Notes

**Why the drawer and not a wider row.** A row that grows to hold the evidence
turns the list into a list of essays, and the owner's job is to decide, not to
read. A drawer is opened when the owner asks to see, and it does not change
what the list looks like when they do not.

**Why the evidence is also beside the work.** The decision answers "do I want
this?"; the work answers "what is it?". If only the decision carries the
evidence, the owner has to go looking for the context every time they come back
to it.
