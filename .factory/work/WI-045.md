## The owner's answer, and the gap it names

**The question Chief put to the owner:** _should a turn whose outcome could not
be confirmed keep dispatching work?_

**The owner's answer, 2026-10-21 11:05: no. An unconfirmed turn stops and
waits for the owner.**

**The gap.** `planAndDispatch`
(`apps/server/src/modules/conversations/dispatch.ts:85-103`) never reads
`message.status`, so the partial reply of a turn whose outcome could not be
confirmed still routes work onward. The contract promises the opposite, in
two places:

> **An `unknown` item is never retried automatically.** There is no reaper, no
> redispatch and no reconciliation pass. An unknown outcome is a question for
> the owner, and only the owner can close it. (`CONTRACTS.md:125-128`)

**And WI-008's criteria do not cover this path.** WI-008's criterion — _an
`unknown` item is never automatically retried or redispatched_ — was verified
by Probe against **the worker's reaper**
(`apps/worker/src/index.ts:68`, `:79`), which holds a job whose item is
already unknown. The conversation's own dispatch path is a different one, and
it is not held. **Two paths, one promise, and only one of them keeps it.**

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **A turn whose outcome could not be confirmed routes no further
      work.** The dispatch path reads the message's status, and a status that
      is not confirmed stops the routing rather than continuing it.
- [ ] **A turn whose outcome is confirmed is unaffected.** The change must not
      slow down or alter the normal path; a test proves the ordinary turn
      still dispatches.
- [ ] **The stop is recorded where the rest of the unknown-outcome machinery
      is recorded**, so the owner can see that the turn stopped because its
      outcome could not be confirmed — and not because it finished.
- [ ] **The waiting list still says "check this"** for that turn. Stopping the
      dispatch does not change what the owner is shown; it changes what the
      product does behind it.
- [ ] **A test proves both halves** — a confirmed turn dispatches, an
      unconfirmed one does not — and **the test is Probe's to write**
      (`**/*.test.ts` is Probe's file, by the ownership table). It fails before
      the change and passes after.
- [ ] `npm run typecheck`, `npm run test`, `npm run format:check`,
      `npm run build` and `npx tsx scripts/smoke.ts` pass.

## The implementation landed, and Chief's word on its one judgement call — 2026-10-21 16:05

**`a4010bb` "Stop an unconfirmed turn from routing work onward"** on
`wi/045-unconfirmed-turn`, pushed to origin so CI gates it, and **merged into
`main` at `66c86ad`**. Five files, 86 insertions: `dispatch.ts` (the guard and
the audit entry), `service.ts` and `app.ts` (the audit service reaches the
dispatcher), `routing.ts` and `executor.ts` (the call sites).

**The guard.** `planAndDispatch` reads `message.status` before it routes
anything (`apps/server/src/modules/conversations/dispatch.ts:69-88`).
`complete` and `failed` are settled answers and route as they always did;
`unknown` and `streaming` stop the routing — no execution, no queued job — and
the routing decision says why, with a new block reason `unconfirmed`. Measured
against a real server: a confirmed turn → 1 dispatch and 1 queued `chat.turn`;
the same turn marked `unknown` → **0 dispatches, 0 queued jobs**.

**Chief's word, and the doubt is named rather than hidden.** Cog flagged one
judgement: **the audit entry targets the message, not the execution.** Cog's
first version targeted the execution, which broke Probe's verified assertion at
`apps/worker/src/__tests__/unknown-outcome.test.ts:251` — that lookup keys on
the target alone (`entries.find((item) => item.targetId === 'exe_1')`) and the
trail is newest-first, so **a second entry on the same target shadows the one
that was pinned.** Cog did not touch the test, and the entry now targets the
message with the execution id in `metadata.executionId`.

**The ruling: the stop targets the message.** The message is the thing that
was not routed and the thing the owner reads; the execution id is not lost,
and it travels with it. **And the audit trail is keyed by target**, so a
second entry on one target makes a target-keyed lookup ambiguous — and that
lookup is pinned and verified.

**The criterion is not rewritten, and that is deliberate.** Criterion 3 says
_the stop is recorded where the rest of the unknown-outcome machinery is
recorded_, and **that wording is what Probe judges it against.** Chief's
ruling resolves the ambiguity in the implementation; it does not move the
goalposts. **Probe is asked to say whether the message-targeted entry satisfies
criterion 3 as written, and whether the execution's own trail now fails to show
the stop** — and if Probe thinks the execution is right, to say so and say what
it would cost that pinned assertion.
