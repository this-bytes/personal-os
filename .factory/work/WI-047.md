## The owner's answer, and the gap it names

**The owner's answer, 2026-10-21: five domains, declared before they are moved.**

The gap: Personal OS has thirteen server modules and seven web pages, and that
layout says where the files are, not which parts of the product may evolve
independently. Two features that both need the same table have no way to be
developed in parallel without a collision, and no way to be reasoned about
separately.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] **`docs/domains.md` names every module, table, contract and owner** for
      each of the five domains, and nothing is left unclaimed.
- [ ] **The three rules are stated**: ownership, one spine, one sanctioned
      crossing — and the crossing rule says what a declared crossing is and
      what it costs.
- [ ] **The three judgement calls are recorded with their reasons**:
      `capabilities` is Substrate rather than Runtime, because it is the
      sanctioned crossing; `events` and `audit` are Substrate, because the spine
      every domain depends on cannot live inside one of them; `automations` is
      Resources rather than Runtime, because an automation is one of the six
      resource kinds.
- [ ] **One line distinguishes "domains" from "the three pillars"** in
      `docs/strategy.md`, so the two vocabularies do not collide.
- [ ] **The map says what it costs** — that the test proves the module boundary
      and the table claims and not more, and that one table write across a
      boundary is declared rather than enforced.
- [ ] **The map is declarative**, and says so: it names the boundaries and does
      not move files.
